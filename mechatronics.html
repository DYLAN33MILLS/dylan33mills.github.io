The challenge

Find the color.
Navigate the arena.

Our six-person team built a robot for a balloon-popping competition. The arena combined colored balloons, walls, and a white-tape boundary, requiring perception and movement to work together.

The architecture split the problem into two subsystems: a local vision-tracking turret and a mobile platform designed to detect obstacles, stay within the boundary, and approach the assigned balloon color.

My focus: turret controls & embedded integration.

  • Developed C++ tracking controls using a Pixy2 camera and Arduino.
  • Iterated the turret actuation from servo to stepper to DC motor.
  • Tuned feedback control to balance tracking response, overshoot, and oscillation.
  • Integrated sensing, motor control, and the rotating mechanical assembly.
System architecture

Two systems.
One physical robot.

Each subsystem handled its own sensing and actuation. The turret tracked locally while the platform managed the robot’s movement through the arena.

Turret subsystem

Local tracking loop

  1. Pixy2 cameraRecognizes trained balloon colors
  2. Arduino UnoProcesses vision input and feedback control
  3. Rotating turret assemblyDC motor provides continuous rotation

Keeping the turret’s controller and power on the rotating assembly helped avoid cable winding during rotation.

Mobile platform

Perception and movement

  1. Camera + ultrasonic + IRColor, obstacle, and boundary inputs
  2. Raspberry Pi → Arduino UnoPerception and navigation → motor commands
  3. Four-wheel mecanum driveForward, reverse, strafe, and rotation

The platform used I²C during development and USB serial in the final integration.

Iteration & control

The right motion
took more than one motor.

The turret progressed through three actuator approaches as the team balanced available hardware, controllability, and the need for continuous rotation.

  1. 01 / SERVO

    Establish the loop.

    Early camera and servo experiments connected visual detection to physical motion and provided a starting point for controller development.

  2. 02 / STEPPER

    Explore position control.

    A stepper prototype used AccelStepper, image-based feedback, and a limit-switch input while the turret design evolved.

  3. 03 / DC MOTOR

    Enable continuous rotation.

    A DC motor enabled continuous rotation. Iterative tuning focused on tracking response while reducing overshoot and oscillation.

Testing beyond the first response.

Testing checked tracking across operating states and verified that the controller retained the selected balloon color.

Make the interfaces work.

Separating the subsystems simplified local development. Integration still depended on practical details: wiring, available power, component mounting, and communication timing.

The mechanical design brings together the chassis structure, motor and sensor mounts, and turret assembly.

Chassis design iterations
Design stageChange and purpose
Initial conceptAn 8 × 8 in chassis with a central spine and motor mounts worked within the available print-bed size.
Modular expansionA 10 × 12 in layout separated front and rear base plates and added dedicated electronics, wheel, and sensor mounts.
Packaging refinementAn 8 × 9 in iteration adjusted the motor position and chassis clearance to meet balloon-mounting constraints.
Final integrationProtective casing, revised turret mounting, three ultrasonic and three IR sensors, connecting axles, and a rigid spine supported the completed assembly.
